# Prompt: Friday "Courage & Kindness" post (draft)

## Purpose
A weekly variant of "Humanity Moment of the Day" (Fridays): instead of a
quote from the humanities, the center of the post is a **real act of
bravery or kindness** from history or the news. The tone, voice, card
format and "AI for Society Online" byline stay the same as
`generate_post.md`. Only the source material changes.

Status: draft (2026-10-14), not yet used for a published post. Revise
after the first few runs.

## Where to find stories

Prefer sources that have already verified the facts:

- **Carnegie Hero Fund** (carnegiehero.org): investigated, documented
  awards for civilian heroism, announced several times a year.
- **Yad Vashem, Righteous Among the Nations**: people who risked their
  lives to save Jews during the Holocaust, each case formally
  researched.
- **Nobel Peace Prize laureates**, **Profile in Courage Award**
  recipients, and similar honors with a public citation.
- **Anniversaries** of well-documented acts (e.g. Chiune Sugihara's
  visas, July–August 1940; Rosa Parks, Dec 1, 1955). Check what falls on
  or near the posting date first.
- **Solutions journalism** (e.g. Reasons to be Cheerful) for recent,
  reported stories.

Avoid viral clips, "heartwarming" aggregator accounts, and anything with
only one secondhand source. Those are where hoaxes and garbled details
live.

## Rules

1. **Public record only.** Feature people whose act is already part of
   the public record: honorees, historical figures, or people named in
   reporting where they took part willingly. Never build a post around a
   private person's viral moment, a minor who isn't named in an official
   citation, or anyone who appears to want privacy.
2. **One primary source per fact.** Every factual claim in the post
   (names, ages, places, what happened) must trace to the award
   citation, the institution's own record, or original reporting.
   Record those links in `post.md`'s source note.
3. **Handle death with care.** Many Carnegie medals are posthumous.
   It's fine to feature them, but state plainly that the person died.
   Never frame a death as "inspiring," and never use a grieving family's
   words beyond what they made public.
4. **No business lesson.** Same rule as the daily posts: don't turn
   courage into "what this teaches us about leadership."
5. **Let the act carry the post.** Describe what the person did
   concretely and briefly; skip the adjectives ("heroic," "incredible").
   Specifics are more moving than praise.

## Optional pairing

If a line from literature, music or art genuinely fits the act, the
card can carry that line (attributed as usual), with the story in the
post text. This keeps the Friday post visibly part of the same series.
Don't force it: a story with no fitting quote can carry the card on its
own, with a short factual headline instead of a quote.

## Output format

```
STORY:
<who, what, where, when: 2-3 plain sentences, with primary source links>

CARD TEXT:
<either a paired quote with attribution, or a one-line factual headline>

IMAGE DIRECTION:
<as in generate_post.md: evoke the mood, don't illustrate literally;
never a photo of the actual people unless it's public domain or licensed
for this use>

POST TEXT:
<the story in 2-4 short lines, then 1-2 lines of commentary, then
"— AI for Society Online", then #HumanityMomentDaily>

REPOST COMMENT:
<2-4 sentences, first person, per generate_post.md step 6>

ALT TEXT:
<per generate_post.md step 4>
```
