## Source note

Nina Simone, answering "What does freedom mean to you?" in a filmed 1968
interview for New York public television. The footage appears in Liz
Garbus's documentary *What Happened, Miss Simone?* (2015). Wording
corroborated via Duke Magazine and Vice. She goes on: "It's just a
feeling..." — card uses only the opening line.

Repost comment facts (verified 2026-10-14, per ninasimone.com and PBS
American Masters, plus Wikipedia): auditioned for the Curtis Institute
on 7 April 1951 and was rejected (3 of 72 piano applicants accepted that
year; she believed race was the reason, Curtis denied it); began playing
Atlantic City clubs in 1954, was told she'd have to sing to keep the
job, and took the name "Nina Simone" so her mother wouldn't learn she
was playing "the devil's music"; Curtis awarded her an honorary degree
in April 2003, and she was told just days before she died.

Selection: first woman musician in the series since Martha Graham
(dance); music last on 10-02 (Miles Davis). Recent run: Carson,
García Márquez, Harjo, Kurosawa.

Commentary move: craft (last was biographical).

## Personal profile repost comment

Nina Simone wanted to be a concert pianist. The Curtis Institute turned her down in 1951, and she ended up playing Atlantic City bars, where she was told she'd have to sing to keep the job. She took the stage name so her mother wouldn't find out. Curtis gave her an honorary degree in 2003; she learned about it days before she died.
