## REPOST COMMENT

The word "evolution" never appears in the first edition of the Origin. The very last word of the book, "evolved," was the first time Darwin used the word in any form in print.

And the barnacles: they look like shellfish and were long classed with molluscs, but they're crustaceans, kin to crabs and shrimp. Their swimming larvae gave that away in 1830, and Darwin's monographs helped make the case. Genetics and those same larval stages are still redrawing the barnacle family tree today, in a 2022 genome study that opens by mentioning Darwin: https://doi.org/10.1093/gigascience/giac021

Repost comment sources (added 2026-11-24):
- Darwin Correspondence Project, "Darwin's study of the Cirripedia": barnacles
  were classed with molluscs following Linnaeus and Cuvier "until the
  publication in 1830 of John Vaughan Thompson's account" of their larvae;
  Thompson's view "was by no means immediately accepted" (Owen denied it in
  1843).
  https://darwinproject.ac.uk/commentary/life-sciences/darwin-and-barnacles/darwin-s-study-cirripedia
- Linda Hall Library, "John Vaughan Thompson":

- Bernot, Avdeyev, Zamyatin, Dreyer, Alexeev, Pérez-Losada & Crandall,
  "Chromosome-level genome assembly, annotation, and
  phylogenomics of the gooseneck barnacle Pollicipes pollicipes,"
  GigaScience 2022 (doi 10.1093/gigascience/giac021). Abstract: barnacles
  "have fascinated biologists, including Darwin, for centuries"; the
  phylogeny "alters our understanding of barnacle larval evolution"; nearly
