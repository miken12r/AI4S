## POST TEXT

"I believe that we learn by practice. Whether it means to learn to dance by practicing dancing, or to learn to live by practicing living, the principles are the same."
— Martha Graham, This I Believe

Graham didn't begin training until she was 16. She went on to change modern dance.

In a short essay read on Edward R. Murrow's radio series in the 1950s, she didn't make it sound glamorous: "There is fatigue so great that the body cries even in its sleep. There are times of complete frustration. There are daily small deaths."

It takes about ten years, she said, to make a mature dancer. Practice means to perform over and over again, in the face of all obstacles, "some act of vision, of faith, of desire."

Nobody gets to skip the practice. Not even at living.
