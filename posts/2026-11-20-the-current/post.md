## STORY

On October 10, 1991, folk singer Gamble Rogers, 54, went into rough
surf at Flagler Beach, Florida, with an air mattress to reach a swimmer
pulled out by a strong current. Both men drowned; the man who went in
with him reached shore. Rogers was awarded the Carnegie Medal.

## POST TEXT

Swimming was dangerous for him. He went into the Atlantic after a stranger anyway.

Gamble Rogers was a Florida folk singer and storyteller who played folk festivals and coffeehouses across the country.

On October 10, 1991, he was camping at Flagler Beach when a 48-year-old swimmer was pulled out by a strong current. The waves were about six feet high, with no lifeguards. Rogers, 54, waded in with an air mattress, and another man went with him.

A wave carried the swimmer farther out. Another knocked both rescuers off the mattress. The other man made it back. Rogers and the swimmer drowned.

His brother later said an arthritic condition had fused Rogers' spine as a teenager, so swimming in those conditions was dangerous for him. He received the Carnegie Medal, and the park now bears his name.
