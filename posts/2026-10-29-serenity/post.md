Source note: Luis Barragán, acceptance speech for the 1980 Pritzker
Architecture Prize, as published by the prize (PDF, © The Hyatt
Foundation): https://www.pritzkerprize.com/sites/default/files/inline-files/1980_Acceptance%20Speech.pdf
(linked from https://www.pritzkerprize.com/laureates/1980). Verified
verbatim: "Serenity. Serenity is the great and true antidote against
anguish and fear, and today, more than ever, it is the architect's duty
to make of it a permanent guest in the home, no matter how sumptuous or
how humble." Also from the speech: publications "have banished from
their pages the words Beauty, Inspiration, Magic, Spellbound,
Enchantment, as well as the concepts of Serenity, Silence, Intimacy and
Amazement"; the section headings (Beauty, Silence, Solitude, Serenity,
Joy, ...); "in my fountains, silence sings"; and the lava-field "jewels"
passage used in the repost comment. Note: the often-quoted "Any work of
architecture which does not express serenity is a mistake" is NOT in
this speech, so it isn't used.

## POST TEXT

"Serenity is the great and true antidote against anguish and fear."
— Luis Barragán, accepting the Pritzker Architecture Prize, 1980

He went on: it is the architect's duty to make serenity "a permanent guest in the home, no matter how sumptuous or how humble."

He complained that architecture publications had banished words like Beauty, Magic, Enchantment, Silence and Amazement. Then he organized his speech around several of them. Of his fountains, he said: "silence sings."

Serenity as a duty, not a luxury: something owed to the people who will live with what you make.

## REPOST COMMENT

In the same speech, Barragán describes walking the volcanic rock south of Mexico City and coming across small green valleys hidden among the lava, which the shepherds called "jewels." He set out to make gardens there "to humanize, without destroying, its magic." It's the best description of good design I've read in a while.
