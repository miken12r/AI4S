## STORY

On Feb. 6, 2026, hang glider pilot Stephen Parson, 75, crashed into a
tree in a gully on Mount Prevost, British Columbia, and hung injured in
his harness. Matthew Clark, a 24-year-old electrician who had just landed
his paraglider below, climbed up with others, then free-climbed a 27-foot
rock chimney alone to cut him free. Carnegie Medal, announced

## POST TEXT

In February, a 75-year-old hang glider pilot, Stephen Parson, crashed into a tree on Mount Prevost in British Columbia. He hung in his harness in a steep gully with broken bones and a concussion.

Matthew Clark, a 24-year-old electrician, had just landed his own paraglider in a field below and heard it on the radio. He hiked up with others, then climbed the last 27 feet alone: a slippery, mossy rock chimney, no equipment. He cut Parson free, dragged him to safety and gave first aid until a helicopter lifted them both out.

Parson made a full recovery. Clark received the Carnegie Medal last month.

Most courage is this concrete: a distance between you and someone in trouble, and a decision about it.

## REPOST COMMENT

The Carnegie Hero Fund was set up by Andrew Carnegie in 1904 and still requires "conclusive evidence" of an act before it awards a medal. Clark was one of 16 people honored in September. Several of the others died in their rescues, which is worth remembering when you read the ones that end well, like this one.
