# 2026-10-30 — her-own-practice (Courage & Kindness)

## STORY

Navi Pillay, born 1941 in Durban to a family of Indian Tamil origin,
received the 2026 Nobel Peace Prize (announced October 9, 2026) "for
her efforts to promote peace and international law." Under apartheid no
law firm would hire her, so in 1967 she opened her own practice, the
first woman to do so in Natal. In 1973 she won political prisoners on
Robben Island, where Nelson Mandela was held, the right to legal
representation. As a judge on the International Criminal Tribunal for
Rwanda, she helped establish that rape and sexual violence could be
prosecuted as genocide and as crimes against humanity.

## POST TEXT

This month, Navi Pillay was awarded the Nobel Peace Prize "for her efforts to promote peace and international law."

In 1967, under apartheid, no law firm would hire her. They "could not have white employees taking instructions from a coloured person," she later told the BBC. So she opened her own practice, the first woman to do so in Natal.

In 1973 she went to court for political prisoners on Robben Island, where Mandela was held, and won them the right to legal representation.

Years later, as a judge at the Rwanda tribunal, she helped establish that rape could be prosecuted as genocide.

Shut out of the profession, she spent her career opening the law to other people.
