## Source note

Rabindranath Tagore, *Stray Birds* (New York: Macmillan, 1916), no. 82,
"translated from Bengali to English by the author." Verbatim from the
Project Gutenberg text of the 1916 edition (eBook #6524,
https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/6524):
"Let life be beautiful like summer flowers and death like autumn leaves."

Facts:
- The book is dedicated "To T. Hara of Yokohama" (same Gutenberg text).
  Hara Tomitaro (Sankei), a Yokohama silk merchant, hosted Tagore at his
  Sankeien garden for about two and a half months on his first visit to
  Japan, from June 1916 (Japan National Tourism Organization,
  https://www.japan.travel/en/in/travelmustdo/70years/; Keio University,
  https://www.keio.ac.jp/en/about/public-relations/mita-hyoron/foreign-visitors/201704-1).
- Some poems in the collection translate his Bengali *Kanika*; others came
  from the Japan visit (Niyogi Books edition note, https://niyogibooksindia.com/books/stray-birds/).
- 1913 Nobel Prize in Literature, first non-European laureate; songs
  adopted as national anthems of India (1950) and Bangladesh (1972)
  (Britannica, https://www.britannica.com/EBchecked/topic/580333).

Commentary move: juxtaposition (the season).

## Post text

"Let life be beautiful like summer flowers and death like autumn leaves."
— Rabindranath Tagore, Stray Birds (1916)

Tagore wrote this in English himself, in a small book of fragments he called Stray Birds.

Most of us only ask for the first half. He gives both halves the same word, beautiful. Look out a window this week: the trees are making his case.
