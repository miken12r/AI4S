## POST TEXT

"The wisest man I ever knew in my whole life could not read or write."
— José Saramago, Nobel lecture, 1998

He meant his grandfather Jerónimo, a swineherd in the Portuguese village of Azinhaga. On hot summer nights the two of them slept under the fig tree, and Jerónimo told stories until the boy fell asleep asking, "And what happened next?"

When Jerónimo felt death coming, he went out to the yard and said goodbye to his trees, one by one, embracing them.

His grandson worked as a mechanic, became a full-time writer in his fifties, and opened his Nobel lecture with the swineherd. Saramago was born on this day in 1922.

Every storyteller needs someone asking what happened next.

## REPOST COMMENT

In the same lecture Saramago remembers his grandmother Josefa, who on freezing winter nights helped bring the weakest piglets into their bed so they wouldn't die. Late in life, sitting at her door under the stars, she said: "The world is so beautiful and it is such a pity that I have to die."
