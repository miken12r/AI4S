## POST TEXT

"The story is our escort; without it, we are blind."
— Chinua Achebe, Anthills of the Savannah, 1987

In the novel, an old man has come to the capital from a province suffering drought. He says it to the most famous son of his region: the war and the warrior pass, and only the story continues.

Then he turns it around: "Does the blind man own his escort? No, neither do we the story; rather it is the story that owns us and directs us."

We tend to think we tell our stories. He thought it was the other way around.

## REPOST COMMENT

Achebe went 21 years between novels, from A Man of the People in 1966 to this one in 1987, and the Nigerian civil war fell in that gap. When Anthills of the Savannah finally came out, it was shortlisted for the Booker Prize.
