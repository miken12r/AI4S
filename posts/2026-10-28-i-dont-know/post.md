## POST TEXT

"Whatever inspiration is, it's born from a continuous 'I don't know.'"
— Wisława Szymborska, Nobel lecture, 1996

She didn't keep inspiration for poets. She found it in anyone whose work keeps them curious: "doctors, teachers, gardeners – and I could list a hundred more professions."

Then she imagined Newton never saying "I don't know." The apples in his orchard would simply have fallen, and he would have picked them up and eaten them.

We usually say "I don't know" as an apology. She treated it as the place where the work starts.

## REPOST COMMENT

Szymborska's Nobel lecture opens with a confession on behalf of her whole profession: poets admit to being poets only reluctantly. On questionnaires, she said, they tend to write "writer" instead. It's a disarming way to begin, coming from someone who had just won the Nobel Prize in Literature.
