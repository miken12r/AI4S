Verified word for word: "Last January, while sorting through my
storeroom ahead of an imminent move, I came across an old shoe box. I
opened the box to find several diaries dating back to my childhood.
Among the stack of journals, there was a pamphlet, the words 'A Book of
Poems' written in pencil across its front. ... The year – 1979 – and my
name were written on the back of the chapbook ... The lines penned by my
eight-year-old self were suitably innocent and unpolished, but one poem
from April caught my eyes. It opened with the following stanzas: Where is
love? / It is inside my thump-thumping beating chest. / What is love? /
It is the gold thread connecting between our hearts." Also: "the big
metal stapler I had sneaked out from my father's room"; "after learning
that our family would be moving to Seoul"; "the inexplicable feeling of
not wanting to show my 'Book of Poems' to anyone"; "I took a photo of
that poem with my phone. I did this out of a sense that there was a
continuity between some of the words I had written then and who I now
was." "In a flash I was transported back forty years." Closing: "In
these moments I experience again the thread of language that connects
us ... I would like to express my deepest gratitude to all those who
have connected with me through that thread, as well as to all those who
may come to do so."

Sorting through a storeroom before a move, the Nobel laureate found a shoebox of childhood diaries and a booklet titled "A Book of Poems." She photographed this one before closing the lid, sensing a continuity between the words she wrote then and who she is now.

She read it in her Nobel lecture in 2024, and ended that lecture with the same image: the thread of language that connects her to her readers.

More than forty years later, she still agreed with herself.

## REPOST COMMENT

Han Kang made that booklet at eight, after learning her family was moving to Seoul, gathering poems from slips of paper and the margins of her workbooks. She stapled it with a big metal stapler sneaked out of her father's room, and once it was finished, she didn't want to show it to anyone.
