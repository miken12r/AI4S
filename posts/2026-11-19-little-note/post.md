"I should be glad, if I could flatter myself that I came as near to the

## POST TEXT

"The world will little note, nor long remember what we say here, but it can never forget what they did here."
— Abraham Lincoln, Gettysburg, November 19, 1863

Lincoln was the second speaker that day, at the dedication of a cemetery for the soldiers who died at Gettysburg. The famous orator Edward Everett had just spoken for two hours.

The next day Everett wrote to him: "I should be glad, if I could flatter myself that I came as near to the central idea of the occasion, in two hours, as you did in two minutes."

Lincoln got one thing wrong that day. The world did remember what he said. Maybe because he spent his two minutes pointing at someone else.
