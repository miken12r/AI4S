# 2026-10-26 — songs-of-hope

Source note: Mahalia Jackson with Evan McLeod Wylie, *Movin' On Up*
(Hawthorn, 1966). Passage verified word for word in the Internet Archive
scan (https://archive.org/details/movinonup0000maha, search-inside, scan
page 76): "I'll never give up my gospel songs for the blues. Blues are
the songs of despair, but gospel songs are the songs of hope. When you
sing them you are delivered of your burden. You have a feeling that there
is a cure for what's wrong." Repost-comment detail from the same book
(scan page 40): "when she was away cooking at the white folks' house I
played Fred's records all day long—especially the blues songs of Bessie
Smith. Bessie was my favorite, but I never let people know I listened to
her." and "I'd fix my mouth and try to make my tones come out just like
hers." Born October 26, 1911, New Orleans; died January 27, 1972
(birthday hook; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahalia_Jackson).

She wouldn't give up gospel for the blues, but she grew up on them. As a girl in New Orleans, while her aunt was out at work, she played her cousin's Bessie Smith records all day and tried to shape her mouth to make her tones come out like Bessie's. She just never let anyone know she was listening.
