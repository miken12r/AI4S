## Source note

Rachel Carson, *The Sense of Wonder*. The text first ran as "Help Your
Child to Wonder" in *Woman's Home Companion*, July 1956. It was published
as a book by Harper & Row in 1965, the year after Carson's death (April 14,
1964), with photographs by Charles Pratt.

Quote, verbatim: "Those who contemplate the beauty of the earth find
reserves of strength that will endure as long as life lasts." The next
sentence in the text is "There is symbolic as well as actual beauty in the
migration of the birds, the ebb and flow of the tides, the folded bud ready
for the spring."

## Post text

"Those who contemplate the beauty of the earth find reserves of strength that will endure as long as life lasts."
— Rachel Carson, The Sense of Wonder (1956)

Carson wrote this about walks along the Maine shore with a small boy: tide pools, rocks, birds, the stars after dark.

A few years later she was writing Silent Spring while being treated for cancer. The line reads less like comfort than like instructions: go outside, look closely, keep going back.

## Personal profile repost comment

The boy on those walks was Roger Christie, Carson's grandnephew. When his mother died in 1957, Carson adopted him; he was five. The essay came out as a book in 1965, the year after her death, with photographs of the Maine coast.
