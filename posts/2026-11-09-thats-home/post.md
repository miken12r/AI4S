Source note: Carl Sagan, *Pale Blue Dot: A Vision of the Human Future in
Space* (Random House, 1994). The card lines, "Look again at that dot.
That's here. That's home. That's us.", are quoted and attributed to the
book on NASA's own page for the image:
https://science.nasa.gov/resource/voyager-1s-pale-blue-dot/
The book itself couldn't be checked word for word: both Internet Archive
scans (palebluedot00carl, palebluedotv00saga) are lending-only and their
search-inside was failing on 2026-11-09. Only the short excerpt NASA
prints is used; nothing longer from the passage.
Image facts, from JPL, "Pale Blue Dot Revisited" (2020):
https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/pale-blue-dot-revisited
Obtained Feb. 14, 1990, "just minutes before Voyager 1's cameras were
powered off"; Sagan "originated the idea of using Voyager's cameras to
image the distant Earth"; Earth appears as "a single, bright blue pixel."
Distance: about 3.7 billion miles (6 billion km) from the Sun (NASA
science page). Hook: Sagan's birthday, Nov. 9, 1934.

## REPOST COMMENT

The photo on the card shows Sagan beside a model of NASA's Viking Mars lander. For the Pale Blue Dot's 30th anniversary in 2020, NASA reprocessed the image with modern software, with input from two of the people who planned the original shot.
