## Story

On 16 October 1943 German forces rounded up the Jews of Rome; more than a
thousand were deported to Auschwitz. At the Fatebenefratelli hospital on
Tiber Island, across from the ghetto, director Giovanni Borromeo, the prior
Father Maurizio and Dr. Vittorio Emanuele Sacerdoti admitted Jews as
patients with "K disease," a diagnosis that frightened the Germans. Yad
Vashem recognized Borromeo as Righteous Among the Nations on 13 October 2004.

## Source note

- Yad Vashem, Righteous Among the Nations database, "Borromeo Giovanni"
  (primary): https://collections.yadvashem.org/en/righteous/5221268
  Confirms: Borromeo (1898–1961), hospital director; Sacerdoti admitted Jews
  with the approval of Borromeo and Father Maurizio, prior of the
  Fatebenefratelli; others joined them on the day of the 16 October 1943
  mass arrest; "K. disease" could suggest Koch disease (tuberculosis) or
  "Kreps" (cancer), "which terrified the Germans," and was also a
  conventional way of saying Kesselring disease; Sacerdoti worked there on
  falsified documents; recognized 13 October 2004.
- Roundup numbers: about 1,020 deported (Primo Levi Center,
  https://primolevicenter.org/printed-matter/?p=5032); post says "more than a
  thousand."
- Card text: "Whosoever saves a single life, saves an entire universe"
  (Mishnah, Sanhedrin 4:5), the inscription on Yad Vashem's Righteous medal
  (Yad Vashem, "Honoring the Righteous":
  https://yadvashem.org/righteous/about-the-program/honoring-the-righteous.html).
- Repost quote: Sacerdoti to the BBC, "Italian doctor who fooled Nazis,"
  3 December 2004, as cited by the Jewish Virtual Library
  (https://jewishvirtuallibrary.org/roman-hospital-saves-jews-by-inventing-disease).
  The BBC page itself couldn't be fetched; JVL also identifies Sacerdoti as
  a Jewish doctor working under a false name.
- Deliberately omitted: the often-repeated "saved about 100" figure
  (secondary sources only) and the claim that Borromeo alone invented the
  disease (Yad Vashem credits the three together).

83 years ago today, on October 16, 1943, German forces rounded up the Jews of Rome. More than a thousand were deported to Auschwitz.

Across from the ghetto, on Tiber Island, the Fatebenefratelli hospital took in Jews who got away. The director, Giovanni Borromeo, the prior, Father Maurizio, and Dr. Vittorio Sacerdoti admitted them as patients with "K disease." It sounded like tuberculosis, and it frightened the Germans away. Among themselves, the K also stood for Kesselring, the German commander.

The tools were ordinary: an admissions form, a ward, one letter on a chart. Using them was not.

## Personal profile repost comment

The doctor who brought many of them in, Vittorio Sacerdoti, was Jewish himself, working at the hospital under a false name. Sixty years later he told the BBC: "the Nazis thought it was cancer or tuberculosis, and they fled like rabbits." Yad Vashem honored the hospital's director, Giovanni Borromeo, as Righteous Among the Nations in 2004.
