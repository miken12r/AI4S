## STORY

On May 21, 1945, near Shuri on Okinawa, Army medic Desmond Doss was
wounded by a grenade, waited five hours for litter bearers, then crawled
off the litter so they would take a more critically wounded man first.
His Medal of Honor citation records it, along with the rifle stock he
used as a splint.

## CARD TEXT

Badly wounded, he waited five hours for a stretcher.
Then he crawled off it so a worse-hurt man could go first.
— Desmond Doss · Army medic, Okinawa, 1945

## POST TEXT

Okinawa, May 21, 1945. In a night attack near Shuri, Army medic Desmond Doss stayed in the open with the injured until a grenade hit him in the legs.

He treated himself rather than call another medic out of cover, and waited five hours for stretcher bearers. On the way back he saw a more critically wounded man, crawled off the stretcher, and told the bearers to take the other man first. While he waited for them to return, he was hit again.

Doss was a Seventh-day Adventist who chose not to bear arms. The only time his Medal of Honor citation puts a rifle in his hands, it's the stock he bound to his broken arm as a splint before crawling 300 yards to the aid station.

The medal was for bravery. Getting off the stretcher was a kindness, and the citation took care to record it.

## REPOST COMMENT

Earlier in the same fight, Doss's company took about 75 casualties on top of a 400-foot escarpment, and the citation says he stayed up there alone with them, lowering them one by one down the cliff on a stretcher tied to a rope. He did it as a noncombatant. He died in 2006, at 87.
