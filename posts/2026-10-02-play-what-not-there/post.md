## Source note

Quote is widely attributed to Miles Davis, documented via bandmate Dave
Holland's recollection in Ian Carr's 1982 biography *Miles Davis: A
Biography* (William Morrow and Co., p.176), and repeated in SPIN
(Dec 1990) and the Christian Science Monitor (1991). Holland's own gloss
on what Miles meant: "Don't play what your fingers fall into... play
something else." Used as a direct quote — solid secondhand sourcing
from a named bandmate in a published biography.

## Post text

"Don't play what's there, play what's not there."
— Miles Davis

His bandmate Dave Holland said what Miles actually meant was simple: don't reach for the note your fingers already know — the one you'd play without thinking. Play the one you'd have to go looking for.

## Personal profile repost comment

Miles Davis built an entire sound around what he left out — space, silence, the note he didn't play. Worth remembering restraint is a creative choice, not the absence of one.
