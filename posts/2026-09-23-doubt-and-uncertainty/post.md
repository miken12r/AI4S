## Post text

"I can live with doubt and uncertainty and not knowing. I think it's much more interesting to live not knowing than to have answers which might be wrong."
— Richard Feynman, BBC *Horizon* interview (1981)

He spent his life doing physics — the field most obsessed with certainty — and still preferred the not-knowing. Most people treat doubt as a problem to be solved. He treated it as the more interesting option.

The famous "questions that can't be answered vs. answers that can't be questioned" line gets attributed to Feynman constantly — it isn't his. This is what he actually said, on camera, in 1981. Close in spirit, but his own words hold up fine without the fake polish.

## Note on quote

The popular "I would rather have questions that can't be answered..."
line is misattributed to Feynman — traced by Quote Investigator to a
1995 email, not Feynman. Used a verified quote instead, from his 1981
BBC Horizon interview ("The Pleasure of Finding Things Out").
