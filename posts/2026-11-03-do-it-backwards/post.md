# 2026-11-03 — do-it-backwards

Source note: Katherine Johnson, quoted in "She Was a Computer When
Computers Wore Skirts," NASA Langley Researcher News, August 26, 2008:
https://www.nasa.gov/centers/langley/news/researchernews/rn_kjohnson.html
Verified verbatim: "Early on, when they said they wanted the capsule to
come down at a certain place, they were trying to compute when it
should start. I said, 'Let me do it. You tell me when you want it and
where you want it to land, and I'll do it backwards and tell you when
to take off.' That was my forte." The article places this as her
calculating "the trajectory of Alan Shepard's 1961 trip into space,
America's first." Glenn passage, same article: "By the time John Glenn
was to go up to orbit the Earth, NASA had gone to computers. ... 'But
when they went to computers, they called over and said, "tell her to
check and see if the computer trajectory they had calculated was
correct." So I checked it and it was correct.'" Repost quote, same
article: "'I didn't have time for that,' ... 'My dad taught us "you
are as good as anybody in this town, but you're no better."'" Glenn's
orbit was February 1962. The well-known "get the girl" line attributed
to Glenn is NOT in this source, so it isn't used.

## POST TEXT

"You tell me when you want it and where you want it to land, and I'll do it backwards and tell you when to take off."
— Katherine Johnson, NASA mathematician

She was describing Alan Shepard's 1961 flight, America's first trip into space. She calculated his trajectory, working back from where the capsule had to come down.

By the time John Glenn orbited the Earth in 1962, NASA had moved to electronic computers. Then, she said, "they called over and said, 'tell her to check and see if the computer trajectory they had calculated was correct.'"

She checked it. It was correct.

The machines were faster. They still called her.

## REPOST COMMENT

In the same 2008 NASA profile, Johnson said she never doubted she was anyone's equal: "I didn't have time for that." Her father had taught her: "you are as good as anybody in this town, but you're no better." She worked at Langley for 33 years. "Never did I get up and say I don't want to go to work."
