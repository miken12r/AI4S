## Source note

Lorraine Hansberry, *A Raisin in the Sun* (1959), Act III; Mama (Lena
Younger) to her daughter Beneatha. Wording and ellipsis exactly as the
Lorraine Hansberry Literary Trust gives it:
"When you starts measuring somebody, measure him right…Make sure you done
take into account what hills and valleys he come through before he got
wherever he is." (https://www.lhlt.org/node/591)

Facts:
- Opened on Broadway March 11, 1959, at the Ethel Barrymore Theatre; first
  play by a Black woman produced on Broadway; title from Langston Hughes's
  "Harlem." (HISTORY.com, https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/march-11/a-raisin-in-the-sun-debuts-broadway;
  Playbill, https://playbill.com/article/black-history-on-broadway-celebrating-lorraine-hansberrys-a-raisin-in-the-sun)
- Hansberry born 1930, died 1965 (American Academy of Arts & Sciences,
  https://www.amacad.org/person/lorraine-vivian-hansberry); born May 19,
  1930, so she was 28 at the opening.
- Hansberry v. Lee (1940): the family moved into Chicago's Washington Park
  subdivision in 1937, a neighbor sued to enforce a racial covenant, and
  the U.S. Supreme Court ruled unanimously for Hansberry on Nov 12, 1940.
  (Illinois Courts, https://illinoiscourts.gov/News/1133/Illinois-Supreme-Court-history-Racial-covenants/news-detail;
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hansberry_v._Lee)

## Post text

"When you starts measuring somebody, measure him right… Make sure you done take into account what hills and valleys he come through before he got wherever he is."
— Lorraine Hansberry, A Raisin in the Sun (1959)

Mama says this to her daughter in Act III, an hour after the family's money is gone and her son has never looked smaller.

Hansberry gives the play's most careful instruction to the character with the least formal grammar. It's a measurement, and the terrain counts.

## Personal profile repost comment

Hansberry knew the play's central fight from her own childhood. In 1937 her father moved the family into a white Chicago neighborhood covered by a racial covenant; a neighbor sued, and in 1940 the U.S. Supreme Court ruled for the Hansberrys. She was 28 when Raisin opened on Broadway, the first play by a Black woman produced there.
